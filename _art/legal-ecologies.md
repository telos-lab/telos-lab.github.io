# {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-seedling fa-fw" %}Legal Ecologies: Data art inspired by mycelium

## Introduction

Law is a tool that serves many purposes. However, unlike many other tools, it changes its shape depending on *how* and *when* it is being looked at. One cannot grasp its form by considering individual documents in isolation. Instead, the law reveals its true shape when approached from a greater distance. This data-art project explores new ways of seeing *legal ecologies*.

To this end, we take inspiration from nature – specifically, from *mycelium* - a certain part of fungi that plays a vital role in sustaining its own plant and the ecosystem surrounding it. Through a web of tiny threads (*hyphae*), mycelium grows to form an intricate network of interconnected fungi, a crucial web of life beneath the surface.

### Mycelium as a visual language for law

"The law" is primarily perceived *as text*. Documents store information in natural language, or: in language as natural as the particularities of the legal jargon allow. Mycelium suggests a different visual representation of such documents. Instead of focusing on words and characters, it naturally emphasizes *structure*. Every legal document is shown as a *mycelius* (*mycelium* + *ius* [lat. "law"]), where each line represents a text component on a granular level, e.g., a paragraph or even a sub-(sub-)section. These lines are grouped through (sub-)chapters. In other words: What we see is the translation of an extended table of contents into the visual appearance of a mycelium.

A second set of lines shows how the documents are *connected*. *Within* and *across* legal documents, *internal* and *external* references create a web of interconnected legal rules (e.g., in legislation) and arguments (e.g., in jurisprudence). These cross-references are crucial for determining legal meaning, and they form the backbone of legal ecosystems. 

### A growing legal ecosystem

Ecosystems can often only be observed in their current state. It is easy to forget that their *current state* is tied to their *previous states*. Much like mycelium, legal ecosystems, too, are *growing* and *changing*. These dynamics can be observed in our data art when viewed as an animation: As various *mycelii* appear in temporal order, over time, one can see the web of law emerge and evolve.

## Mycelius at COP31

The artworks presented at the Finish Pavilion at COP31 – two videos of ~3m each – show the development of two legal areas concerning sustainability: biodiversity protection and clean energy incentivization. Distributed among multiple documents of various types, their regulatory frameworks grow into veritable *ecosystems*.

### Movement I: Biodiversity Protection

The first movement tells a story on the *conservation of nature*, showing the *Birds Directive* in its judicial habitat. The Birds Directive roots back to 1979 when the EU was still the European Economic Community. It is located at the center of the canvas. Surrounding it are the mycelii of recent court decisions that source from the Birds Directive and simultaneously shape its content in action.

### Movement II: Clean Energy

The second movement targets the *generation of power*, granting insights into several Renewable Energy Directives (I, II, and III) in their regulatory environment. Between these cornerstones of climate legislation, one can see the mycelii of other documents of various kinds: court decisions, so-called implementing regulations, and other directives. Together, these instruments shape how energy is produced in the EU.
